Act III 2:00–3:00

Architecture and Switch

PurposeSwitch vs MCP vs director vs model; the graph; the remove-Switch proof.

Narration

Six layers, one world. Switch is the transport: rooms, one identity per character, delivery, and a Console session you can inspect. It does not hold the world state. The MCP world engine holds the facts: beliefs, provenance, relationships, the scene card. Each character carries a scoped token, so Alice cannot read Emily’s private beliefs — that is enforced at the API, not in the prompt. The director is deterministic Python: it plans the turn, gates the ending, relays one hop. It is not an LLM scheduler. The model only writes the sentence. Bifrost routes; OpenCode is the preferred connector, with Claude Code as the live fallback here. Now remove Switch. Rooms, sessions, delivery, live beats — gone. What remains: the world database, the dashboard projection, the phone DM, the local beat runner, and the scene card.

Act IV 3:00–3:50

Why a game, not a business case

PurposeThe market gap, stated carefully: multi-agent as caller in a playable world.

Narration

Most AI NPC demos end the same way: one character, one player, one conversation. NVIDIA ACE, Convai and Inworld build characters answering a player. AutoGen, CrewAI and LangGraph automate tasks, not lives. The closest counterexamples are prototypes or story engines: Stanford’s twenty-five generative agents, Altera’s Minecraft society of up to a thousand agents, Hidden Door’s ensemble worlds. We did not find one that documents the combination: a small cast that can address each other, hold private memories, and respect boundaries with provenance. That is the gap. We built it as a game because a game is the cheapest testbed — stakes, a room, a witness. A business case would turn the cast into a workflow; a game keeps the stakes and gives the research a story people can feel.

01 A · Agent 0:30–0:52 · 22s

One agent, one voice, one private thread.

Exact action
Story → load w7-last-call, scene 1 truck-dusk (Mia only). Hold her CG and nameplate. Open the phone (Mia is already a friend in w7), type “You’re the only person I can say this to.”, let the reply land. Close; Mia’s cast card → Profile; hold the full-body art, quote, relationship bars, 4/5 hearts.
What it proves
Each character is a separately modeled agent with a persistent private voice and a portrait identity, not a mask on one shared prompt. At four hearts the contact contract is limited, not refused.
Fallback
Pre-warm the DM. If the model misses, hold the profile sheet and a still of the reply. Never wait more than 5 seconds.
02 B · Multi-agent 0:52–1:15 · 23s

One room, not four chatbots: lead, reactor, silence.

Exact action
Story → load w1-gift so all four are present. Type “Mia, what do you think of it?” Hold the turn log: “Mia takes the lead · Alice reacts · Emily, Sophia stay quiet.” Hold the dimmed silence rows. If a reply contains @name, hold the “reply to <name>” note and the relayed reply.
What it proves
The director plans the room before any model call — one primary, one reactor, recorded silence — so it is a room, not four parallel chatbot replies. A mention, when it occurs, relays exactly one hop in public.
Fallback
The plan and silence rows are deterministic; hold there. For the relay use a 6-second pre-recorded clip. If it is absent, drop the relay and say “one hop” over the plan. Do not retry live.
03 C · Scene next 1:15–1:37 · 22s

Authored take, scene gate: only change moves the story.

Exact action
Worlds → w3-quiet-route, take “The Quiet Route”, scene 1 of 2. Show the gate note “Say a line or let a cue change the scene before advancing.” → Story → click “That skirt in the window — the short one. It’s you.” (cue matches skirt|friday|wear|dress). → Worlds → Next scene → “Scene 2 of 2 loaded.”
What it proves
The story advances through an authored, linear take and a state-change gate, so canon cannot be skipped by prose.
Fallback

04 D · Memory system 1:37–2:00 · 23s

Belief, provenance, hearts: a memory you can point at.

Exact action
Story → scene 2 “The quiet route home” (Alice in the burgundy skirt). 03 / The spine

One world, six layers

Keep the graph on screen as the spine and cut away only to the evidence. Switch owns delivery; the engine owns facts.

Six bands: play surfaces · conductor · Switch transport · world engine · models · offline art

  • Switch

    Transport, not world state. Rooms, one identity per character, delivery, sessions, and a Console session you can inspect.

  • MCP world engine

    Source of truth. Scoped tokens, beliefs, provenance, relationships, the scene card. One token maps to one character: Alice cannot read Emily’s private beliefs at the API, not in the prompt.

  • Director

    Deterministic Python. Plans the turn, gates the ending, relays one hop. Not an LLM scheduler; the model only writes the sentence.

  • Models

    Bifrost routes. OpenCode is preferred; Claude Code is the live fallback. DeepSeek v4.1 Flash. Low reasoning by default; high only for body and contact.

04 / Closing thesis

Why a game, not a business case

The category is thin. The gap is a product-surface inference, not a forecast. No market-size statistic is used.

Most AI NPC demos end the same way: one character, one player, one conversation.

The gap is not another voice. It is a small persistent cast that can address each other inside a playable world — with scoped identity, private memory, and auditable provenance. No public product surface we checked documents the full combination.

Product-surface inference · not a measured market study
01

Character intelligence

They answer you.

NVIDIA ACE, Convai, Inworld. Player-facing conversation and voice; multi-character behavior is ambient, not an auditable social contract.

02

Task automation

They finish tasks.

AutoGen, CrewAI, LangGraph. The unit is a task; there is no world, persistent identity, or social memory.

03

Research simulation

Societies in a sandbox, not a shipped room.

Generative Agents, Project Sid. Proof of the pieces; not one playable loop with scoped identity, private memory and provenance.

+

The open lane

A small persistent cast that can address each other inside a playable world.

Scoped identity, private memory, auditable provenance. No public product surface we checked documents the full combination.

Closest commercial counterexample: Hidden Door documents persistent worlds, ensemble casts, relationships and canon. Its public page does not document agent-to-agent address or per-character private-state provenance.

We built it as a game because a game is the cheapest testbed — stakes, a room, a witness. A business case would turn the cast into a workflow; a game keeps the stakes and gives the research a story people can feel.

Claim discipline Say: “We did not find this combination in the public product surfaces we checked.” Do not say: first, only, nobody, or a market size.
